The invariant

North is up, east is right, the horizon is the outer edge and zenith is the center. Node position comes from NASA's dish-local azimuth and elevation. None of these scopes is a geographic map, inertial craft position, predicted pass or decorative orbit. Pointing is dish-scoped: multiple contacts on one dish share one true cell and resolve through a nonspatial tally or selection page, never invented jitter.

Ten directions

Every preview is an exact 72×16 framebuffer using the production proportional font, DSN palette and qualitative physical LED-gap model.

1 Purest form

Equal triptych

Three permanent 24×16 local scopes. Nothing changes size or location; every current pointing node shares the same grammar.

G1 · M0 · C4center = zenith
Why choose it

Fastest network comparison and strongest spatial memory. Madrid's empty scope remains unmistakably present.

Risk

Four Canberra nodes compete inside one 13-pixel ring; exact dish and craft identity require selection.

2 Focus + context BEST BALANCE

User-invoked focus lens

On deliberate wheel focus, expand the selected site's real scope while the other two remain visible as miniature skies. Never auto-rotate this layout.

Focus: G26 · MMS 2two sites remain visible
Why choose it

The selected site gains room for a readable short identity and separated nodes without losing global context.

Risk

Scope sizes move with selection. Mini scopes show occupancy and geometry, not detailed identity.

3 Live history MOST LIVE

Tracking tails

Keep the equal triptych, but retain the last five distinct pixel-visible source samples behind only the selected pointing head.

5 distinct positions maxtail = observed history
Why choose it

You can watch a real antenna track across its local sky over minutes. This cannot be reproduced honestly from an offline craft list.

Risk

Real DSN pointing often changes slowly; the desk object must dwell long enough for a trail to become meaningful.

4 Physical-panel safe

Eight-sector roses

Deliberately quantize each local sky to eight compass sectors and three elevation zones, using sturdy two-pixel tangential marks.

8 azimuth sectors · 3 elevation zonesdeclared bins
Why choose it

Trades false precision for shapes that survive the dark LED gaps. Exact degrees remain in the picker and Instrument.

Risk

Slow motion appears only when a declared boundary is crossed; several contacts can still share a bin.

5 Rectangular alternative

Horizon raster

Unwrap each local sky: azimuth runs left→right and elevation runs horizon→zenith vertically inside three aligned plots.

x = azimuth · y = elevationshared scale
Why choose it

Uses nearly every pixel for measurable position and makes site-to-site comparison easy on aligned axes.

Risk

It is analytically clearer but loses the intuitive “looking into the sky” quality of a polar scope.

6 Physical sculpture

Antenna ballet

Use each site cell as a tiny antenna yard: the selected real dish leans with elevation while an azimuth nib and contact beads retain the rest.

pose = published elevationnib = azimuth octant
Why choose it

Most directly mimics the physical machines operating on Earth; it reads as a kinetic desk sculpture rather than a chart.

Risk

Only one dish per site gets a full pose. Three coarse tilt states are honest and legible; pretending at finer precision is not.

7 Dense-contact defense

Collision shutter

When records quantize to the same LED, keep their one truthful cell, page its colors on a fixed clock, and show the multiplicity in a nonspatial gutter.

C: 2 records share one cellno invented jitter
Why choose it

Makes overlap explicit without fabricating two pointing positions. The wheel resolves the collocated identities.

Risk

Color paging can look like telemetry. It must freeze with stale data and keep one fixed eight-second loop regardless of occupant count.

8 Transition story

Observed handoff echo

On an unambiguous same-craft site transition, briefly mark the old and new endpoints inside their own scopes—without connecting their unrelated local skies.

Illustrative: G → Cobserved between snapshots
Why choose it

Turns the three-site reason for the DSN into a rare, memorable event driven by the live source.

Risk

The echo is not proof of a scheduled or continuous handoff. It only marks an unambiguous association change observed between snapshots.

9 Identity rhythm

Scope / identity interleave

Show all three scopes for eight seconds, then spend one complete cycle on the selected dish, full friendly name and exact az/el.

Scope phase8 sec + 8 sec
Why choose it

Solves the main polar-scope weakness—anonymous dots—without shrinking the names or permanently sacrificing the overview.

Risk

A random glance sees only one phase. The transition and 1/5 index must make the time multiplexing obvious.

10 Rare-mode geometry

Topology scopes

Keep literal pointing positions, then give arraying, MSPA and DDOR distinct node geometry instead of hiding them in a distant glyph column.

Array · MSPA · DDOR stress fixtureposition remains observed
Why choose it

The interesting network modes become part of the live sky geometry where the affected contact already lives.

Risk

The shapes have a learning curve and can collide. They must supplement—not overwrite—RF direction and band.

Recommended direction: combine 1 + 2 + 3

Keep the Equal Triptych as the persistent Network view. On deliberate selection, use the Focus Lens, and add Tracking Tails only to the selected contact. It preserves spatial memory, gives the chosen contact room, and makes slow observed movement the live proof.

  1. Wheel selects a contact and expands its site after the existing rest delay.
  2. The current head and sampled trail remain source-timestamp driven.
  3. START narration and hold-to-Instrument/Distance keep the established interaction model.
  4. Observed Handoff Echo can remain a rare event treatment across either layout.

Most beautiful alternative

Antenna Ballet is the next physical-test candidate when the goal is a kinetic desk sculpture. It sacrifices precision for a much stronger sense that real machines on Earth are moving.
