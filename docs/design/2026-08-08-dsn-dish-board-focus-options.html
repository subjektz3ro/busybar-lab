Network candidates

Six ways to draw the board

The first is the shipped default. The others are preserved design alternatives, not promises of future production changes.

1 · Shipped default

Explicit dish roster

Use ordinary text semantics: site link total, dish suffix, and an attached count only when a dish owns more than one link.

  • No positional metaphor or learned RF glyph.
  • 34(2) remains legible with LED gaps.
  • Exact TX/RX stays in Focus and Instrument.
2 · Richer RF

Dish + contact chevrons

Every 4×5 badge is one link. Blue > is TX, warm < is RX; two adjacent badges after 34 show two links.

  • Preserves per-link direction and receive band.
  • Still resolves C5 without paging.
  • Requires one small, testable legend.
3 · Literal contacts

Repeat the dish per link

Draw one dish suffix for every live link. Canberra therefore shows five entries and repeats 34.

  • Five tokens visibly equal C5.
  • No count glyph needed.
  • Risk: two 34s can look like two antennas unless explicitly taught.
4 · Capacity context

Fixed antenna fleet

Every known dish keeps a stable slot. Active dishes brighten; inactive dishes remain dim, so the network’s available ground hardware stays visible.

  • Excellent spatial memory and maintenance context.
  • Shows that not every dish is always linked.
  • Weakest at explaining DSS-34’s two simultaneous links.
5 · Summary + target

Global totals with selected dish

Keep site totals permanently at the top, then use the remaining two rows for the selected dish and its link identities.

  • Names are immediately available without another view.
  • Very clear wheel target.
  • Hides the other active dish suffixes, so it is weaker as Network.
6 · Existing rollback

Three Skies

Keep the current renderer as a rollback and as evidence that the underlying alt-az data remains valuable—just at the selected-dish level.

  • Beautiful, live and scientifically grounded.
  • Best answer to “where is a dish aimed?”
  • Not the best first answer to “what is connected?”

Selected-dish candidates

Three ways to make pointing understandable

Every version names the ground dish before showing geometry. A missing angle produces an explicit no-aim state, never an invented point.

A · Shipped Focus

One aim + two link rows

The plot is small but literal. Each craft gets a separate TX/RX row outside it, so one head can never be mistaken for two spacecraft positions.

B · Plainest

Connection tree

Remove the plot entirely and show one selected dish branching orthogonally to its two links. Equal branch lengths carry no distance meaning.

C · Honest fallback

No published aim

If either angle is absent, retain the dish and links but replace geometry with a complete NO AIM label.

Reading contract

Nothing floats without an owner

The page uses the production font, palette and a qualitative 10-lit / 8-gap panel model.

Visual vocabulary

Truth contract

  • Site counts are links, not dishes. C5 can therefore coexist with four dish numbers.
  • The roster contains no spacecraft positions. It is ground-network topology.
  • The Focus marker is DSS-34’s aim. Center is zenith; rim is horizon; north is up.
  • Two links can share one aim. MSPA and source associations do not create a second antenna.
  • NO LINKS is narrow. It means no displayable active spacecraft association in the accepted source snapshot—not idle, powered down, broken or out of view.
  • No fake motion. The board changes only on accepted source data; the heartbeat owns freshness.

Final call

What shipped

Explicit roster is the default

It passes the three-second test without a legend: four Canberra dish numbers, five total links, and DSS-34 visibly owns two.

  • Keep DSN_NETWORK_STYLE=skies as rollback during hardware testing.
  • Use native wheel text immediately; commit Focus only after wheel rest.
  • Preserve Instrument and Distance unchanged.

Keep richer variants as studies

The 4×5 TX/RX badges remain a useful hardware experiment, but they do not replace the complete text roster without a separate legibility and truth review.

  • Do not reintroduce detached counts.
  • Do not make the local-sky plot ambient again.
  • Do not use line length, diagonal beams or sweeping radar as liveness.
